The true cost of minimum payments

A $10,000 credit card balance at 22% APR, paid at minimum payment only, takes approximately 27 years to eliminate and costs over $19,000 in interest alone. Understanding the math is the first step to changing the outcome.

A step-by-step payoff strategy

1

List every debt

Write down each obligation with its balance, interest rate, and minimum payment. Visibility is the beginning of control.

2

Choose your method

The avalanche method (highest rate first) minimizes total interest. The snowball method (smallest balance first) builds momentum and motivation. Both work—pick the one you'll stick to.

3

Find the extra payment

Even $75–$150 per month in additional payments can cut years off a debt timeline. Review your budget for room to redirect.

4

Roll payments forward

When one debt is paid off, redirect its payment to the next. This compounds your progress dramatically over time.

Common budgeting frameworks

The 50/30/20 Rule

Allocate half your after-tax income to needs, 30% to wants, and the remaining fifth to savings and debt repayment. A solid foundation for most households.

Zero-Based Budgeting

Every dollar of income is assigned a job before the month begins. More labor-intensive, but highly effective for those building new financial habits.
